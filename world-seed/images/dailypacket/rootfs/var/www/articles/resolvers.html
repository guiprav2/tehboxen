Three resolvers answer for the whole net — is that a problem?

Almost every name lookup on the net goes through 8.8.8.8, 1.1.1.1 or 9.9.9.9. All three recurse to a single registry.

Infrastructure desk

Type a name into a browser and the question usually travels the same way: your machine asks your router, your router forwards to one of the public resolvers, and the resolver asks ns.registry.net, the box that holds every public zone.

That keeps things simple. It also means a handful of machines see, and could answer for, every lookup on the net.

Opinion: run your own resolver

Your queries are a diary of where you go. Nothing stops you from pointing /etc/resolv.conf at a resolver you run yourself. The fewer people who can read the diary, the better.
